# Detailed findings — Brisbane River (Colmslie) water quality

*Analysis period: 2023-08-04 to 2024-06-27 · 30-minute cadence · ~30,600 usable records*

## 1. Data preparation

- Timestamps parsed to datetime; rows sorted chronologically.
- **280 duplicate timestamps** (a logging quirk where two records share a
  stamp) were reduced to the first occurrence, leaving ~30,614 records.
- No rows had unparseable timestamps.
- Each parameter carries a paired `[quality]` flag column with coded values
  (e.g. `1020`, `1220`, `2010`) that should be consulted before treating any
  individual reading as authoritative.

## 3. Seasonality

Monthly means show the sub-tropical annual cycle clearly:

- **Temperature**: 18.6 °C (Jun) → 28.3 °C (Feb).
- **Dissolved oxygen**: tracks inversely, lowest in the warm months.
- **Salinity**: ~35 PSU in winter/spring, dropping to ~28 PSU in Jan–Feb as
  wet-season freshwater enters the estuary.
- **Turbidity**: rises through autumn and peaks around May (~10.7 NTU).

## 4. Correlation structure

| Pair | Pearson r | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Salinity ↔ Specific Conductance | 1.00 | Same quantity — redundant |
| Temperature ↔ pH | −0.58 | Warmer water is slightly less alkaline here |
| DO ↔ DO (%sat) | 0.66 | Related by construction |
| Temperature ↔ DO | −0.38 | Solubility effect |
| Salinity ↔ Chlorophyll | −0.28 | Fresher (runoff) water carries more chlorophyll |
| Salinity ↔ Turbidity | −0.27 | Fresher water is more turbid (runoff sediment) |

Flow speed shows near-zero correlation with the chemistry, consistent with
tidal flow being an oscillation rather than a driver of composition.

## 5. Diurnal cycle

Averaging by hour of day isolates a clean biological signal:

- **Dissolved oxygen** bottoms out around **06:00** (overnight respiration has
  drawn oxygen down) and peaks around **16:00** (a full day of photosynthesis).
  The daily swing is ~0.8 mg/L.
- **Chlorophyll** peaks later, in the **early evening (~18:00)**.

## 6. Flow regime

Water direction is bimodal, clustering around opposing headings rather than
spreading uniformly — the flood/ebb axis of tidal exchange. Speed ranges widely
(0 to ~90) with periodic slack-water zeros at tide turns.

## 7. Recommended next steps

1. **Join rainfall / river-discharge data** to test the runoff hypothesis for
   the salinity and turbidity excursions directly.
2. **Gap-aware imputation** for DO and temperature before any forecasting.
3. **Tidal harmonic analysis** on water speed/direction to quantify the M2/S2
   constituents and de-tide the chemistry series.
4. **Threshold/anomaly detection** on DO for identifying hypoxic events, using
   time-of-day-adjusted baselines rather than a single flat cutoff.
